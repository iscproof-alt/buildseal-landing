How VEDM, A9 and A10 relate to the agent handoff

VEDM — Verifiable Evidentiary Decision & Memory Layer. Organizes recorded context, evidence references and decision history across agent steps. The application still has to capture the relevant inputs and events.

A9 · Commitment and finalization

Distinguishes the commitment outcome, COMMITTED or DENIED, from signature and timestamp finalization. A recorded commitment alone is not proof that an external action was executed or that sealing finished.

A10 · Evidence sufficiency

Records an assessment as SUFFICIENT or INSUFFICIENT for specified requirements. A transition can reference the assessment ID and hash. An insufficient result can lead to a recorded request for evidence, escalation or revalidation.

At a handoff: retain the prior record reference, the evidence assessment reference and the commitment outcome as separate facts. They answer different questions: what context was recorded, what evidence was assessed, and what commitment was recorded.

An A10 assessment does not certify that inputs are true or a decision is correct. An A9 outcome is not a cryptographic verification verdict. The relay simulation above does not run these backend mechanisms; its green indicators are simulated. Inspect actual hosted record checks →

One decision

Your application supplies the record.

  1. Your application decides

    A KYC rejection, a credit denial or a fraud flag. Any decision you may need to explain later.

  2. BuildSeal seals the record

    Your application sends the decision, a reason code and input references. The payload is canonicalized, hashed, signed with Ed25519 and submitted for the service’s evidence workflow. Inspect timestamp verification separately.

  3. A reviewer verifies it

    Share the verify link or the evidence package. The reviewer checks that the files still match the signed record.

BuildSeal seals what your application reports. It does not observe the model itself.

Scope

What verification covers, and what it does not.

A sealed record shows what was captured and that it has not changed since. It does not show that what was captured is true.

Applicable verification can check

  • Whether the sealed files still match their recorded hashes.
  • Whether the signature verifies against the signer key.
  • Whether the RFC 3161 timestamp token matches the sealed hash.
  • Linked record references, when supported by the applicable verification profile.
  • Whether a listed file is missing or changed.

Verification does not check

  • Whether a decision was correct or fair.
  • Whether the recorded inputs were accurate.
  • Whether a policy was actually in force.
  • Whether an agent really ran the way its record says.
  • Who the signer is, unless you hold the signer's key from another source.
  • Whether your system meets a legal requirement.

Regulation

Support for logging and audit requirements.

Article 12 of the EU AI Act requires high-risk AI systems to allow automatic recording of events. Tamper-evident records can support that requirement. They do not make a system compliant on their own.

The Digital Omnibus, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the date for most stand-alone high-risk systems (Annex III) from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027. Check which obligations apply to your system. This page is not legal advice.
